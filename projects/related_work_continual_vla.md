# Related work for sequential task addition to a VLA: what exists, what a reviewer will ask for, and what to do about it

Compiled 3 September 2026 from four parallel literature sweeps (continual imitation learning and continual VLA papers; parameter-efficient and memory-based continual learning from the LLM and vision literature; retrieval, in-context and skill-library approaches; surveys, benchmarks and metric conventions). Roughly 90 searches and about 150 paper fetches in total, with the top papers in each slice read in full. Every citation below was fetched by at least one sweep. Items marked **[verify]** were seen only at abstract or snippet level, or the sweeps disagreed on them, and must be checked against the PDF before they go into a submission.

The question asked: does anything in the literature solve "a robot runs a VLA; this week add a task, next week another, the week after another, without forgetting"?

---

## 1. The direct answer

**No published method occupies our exact cell.** The cell is the conjunction of five constraints: tasks arrive one at a time; the backbone is frozen; no oracle task identifier selects parameters at inference; no replay of earlier tasks' data in any form; and the parameter footprint does not grow with the number of tasks. Every method found relaxes at least one of these, and most relax two or three. The single method that satisfies all five is Meta's sparse memory finetuning, and it is a language-model paper.

**But the neighbourhood is crowded.** In 2026 alone, at least six papers do continual learning on a VLA on LIBERO, several on π₀ or π₀.₅ itself, and three of them are task-ID-free and frozen-backbone. A claim of the form "no prior work does continual VLA adaptation without task identity" will be rejected. The defensible claim is the conjunction, and in particular the last two constraints: zero retained data and a fixed parameter budget.

**Product-key memory layers in a robot policy are genuinely unclaimed.** Three sweeps independently ran structured arXiv queries for memory layers or product-key memory combined with robot, policy, manipulation or VLA and found nothing. All 18 papers citing Meta's sparse memory finetuning are language, retrieval or recommendation work. "Memory" in the VLA literature means something different (within-episode temporal context: MemoryVLA, MemER, EventVLA, KEMO, AURA and others), and the paper must disambiguate from that cluster explicitly.

**No retrieval, in-context or skill-library method has ever been evaluated with a forgetting metric**, at any task count. The closest, ReCAP (ICLR 2026), adds tasks to a frozen policy and never re-measures earlier ones. There is therefore no published comparison table from that family; the comparison points have to be generated here.

## 3. The 2026 counter-narrative and how our own data answers it

Three strong groups now argue that large pretrained VLAs barely forget, so the first reviewer objection will be "what problem are you solving?". The answer is in our own tables and in four independent published numbers.

**Simple Recipe Works (Stone and Martín-Martín's groups).** Sequential LoRA on a VLA with on-policy RL gives negative backward transfer of 0.3% on LIBERO-Spatial and beats most dedicated methods. Their Table 3 ablation replaces RL with supervised fine-tuning in the identical setup and the number becomes 78.7% forgetting with average success 29.9%. Their result is a property of on-policy updates, not of VLAs or LoRA. We are in the demonstration regime with no environment reward. Quote that table. Their footnote 2 is also the sharpest statement of the task-ID problem: since the policy is conditioned on the instruction, the instruction is observable, and in LIBERO the instruction uniquely identifies the task.

**Pretrained VLAs are Surprisingly Resistant to Forgetting (Yuke Zhu's group).** The sweeps disagreed on this paper and the disagreement matters. The near-zero forgetting for π₀ is the experience-replay row. The sweep that read the PDF tables reports the plain sequential row on LIBERO-10 at success 0.644 with negative backward transfer 0.562, versus the replay row at 0.586 and −0.070. Their π₀ is LoRA on a frozen backbone, so their sequential row is essentially our naive sequential LoRA baseline, and it forgets. **[verify against the PDF table before quoting.]** Two further defences: they provide the task ID, and the real-world π₀.₅ paper below shows the replay conclusion inverts on heterogeneous real data.

**The real-world π₀.₅ paper.** Ten sequential real tasks, our base model. Naive sequential full fine-tuning drops average score from 86.9 to 31.4 with backward transfer −81.0; replay at 20% recovers to 97.2. On a heterogeneous stream (interleaved single-arm and bimanual) backward transfer stays negative even with replay. This is the strongest published evidence that the problem is real on π₀.₅ with real data.

**Our own corroboration.** Naive sequential LoRA collapses in every configuration we ran: 18.0 at five tasks (rank 256, four seeds), 9.7 at ten tasks (rank 512, nine tasks at exactly 0.0), and function drift of +2356% on the real WidowX chain. Four published numbers sit in the same band: CLARE's sequential LoRA at 21.4 AUC and 71.6 forgetting on LIBERO-Long; the π₀ sequential row above at 0.562; OrthoSkillVLA's sequential LoRA at 32.4% final success; and the real-world 31.4. The paper should lead with "we reproduce the collapse on our own setup" rather than citing it, because a reviewer will otherwise suspect an under-tuned baseline.

## 4. The mechanism's lineage, and what to attribute where

**Continual Learning via Sparse Memory Finetuning** (Lin, Zettlemoyer, Ghosh, Yih, Markosyan, Berges, Oğuz; FAIR and Berkeley; arXiv 2510.15103) is the parent of our method and must be cited as such. Verified mechanism: one FFN replaced by a memory lookup with 1M slots, 32 accesses per token, four heads; slot ranking by TF-IDF where term frequency is the access count on the current batch and document frequency is measured against 1,000 fixed background batches of pretraining data; gradient flows only to the values of the top-t slots via a straight-through mask; t is 500 for facts and 10,000 for documents. Results: after learning 1,000 facts, NaturalQuestions F1 drops 89% with full fine-tuning, 71% with LoRA, 11% with sparse memory finetuning. Their ranking "makes no assumption about task boundaries". No code released.

Three things in that paper bear directly on ours:

- **The TF-only ablation.** Term-frequency ranking without the IDF term learns equally but forgets more, and the gap widens as t shrinks. Our Entry 19 found that at our scale the IDF term is nearly inert (a 60:1 document-frequency ratio becomes a 3× penalty), and our answer was the prior-usefulness protection term (1−u)^β with corefrac normalisation, which structurally excludes earlier tasks' cores. That is the natural generalisation of their fixed background corpus to a growing task stream, and they list it as future work. Frame protection that way.
- **Optimiser.** They use SGD, not Adam, for memory values, and warn that Adam's per-parameter step sizes and momentum "interact with sparsity in unexpected ways". Our log has two independent Adam-statefulness defects on the protection path (Entries 42 and 51). Worth one sentence and possibly one ablation.
- **Their write budget t is the same knob as our top_t.** Two of the four sweeps assumed we did not use TF-IDF slot selection and recommended adding it. We have used it since Phase A; the recipe's top_t 3072 (1536 on the real robot) is that mask. The point for the paper is to name it after Lin et al. and cite it, not to build it.

Follow-ups: **Improving Sparse Memory Finetuning** (Goyal et al., arXiv 2604.05248) retrofits memory layers into a pretrained Qwen-0.5B with a "healing" stage and a KL-divergence slot-selection rule, and uses the phrase "write masks"; **Sparse Memory Finetuning as a Low-Forgetting Alternative to LoRA and Full Finetuning** (Gupta et al., arXiv 2605.03229) is an independent reproduction comparing KL and TF-IDF selection; **Fast-weight Product Key Memory** (arXiv 2601.00671) does test-time gradient updates on activated slots.

**Architecture attribution.** Product-key retrieval is Lample et al., NeurIPS 2019 (arXiv 1907.05242). The SiLU-gated output projection (`output = (y ⊙ silu(xᵀW₁))ᵀW₂`) is from **Memory Layers at Scale** (Berges et al., arXiv 2412.09764), which also explicitly defers continual learning to future work. Cite both for the module; the novel parts of ours are the rank-2 LoRA slot values, the frozen-router-plus-warm-up protocol, frozen-base routing for stationarity, the protection term, the two-tower placement with pooled state-text routing on the VLM side, and shared tables.

**Two other papers that strengthen the thesis.** Task-Specific Skill Localization (Panigrahi, Saunshi, Zhao, Arora, ICML 2023, arXiv 2302.06600): about 0.01% of parameters carry over 95% of a fine-tuned task's performance, and the regions for different tasks are near-disjoint. Reflecting on the State of Rehearsal-free Continual Learning with Pretrained Models (Thede et al., CoLLAs 2024, arXiv 2406.09384): prompt-pool methods work not because of their query mechanism but because of "the implicit bound on tunable parameters". Both are published priors in favour of a sparse write budget, and our Entry 51 top-p result (write everything you read: 19.2%) is the empirical version.

**The model-editing analogue.** GRACE (Hartvigsen et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2211.11031) keeps weights frozen and a discrete key-value adaptor that adds, expands or splits entries with no task ID; 1,000 sequential edits with 137 keys. Its retention metrics (retention on original data, retention of previous edits) map onto old-task versus new-task success. MEMIT-style direct weight editing scales in batch but collapses when applied sequentially (Gupta, Rao, Anumanchipalli, ACL Findings 2024, arXiv 2401.07453), a peer-reviewed one-line argument for a non-parametric store. WISE (arXiv 2405.14768) shards edits into parameter subspaces and claims an "impossible triangle" of reliability, generalisation and locality, which cuts against us and should be engaged.

## 5. Retrieval, in-context and skill-library approaches: why none is a comparator

These are the methods behind the reviewer question "why not just retrieve or prompt?".

- **In-context imitation** (ICRT, arXiv 2408.15980; Instant Policy, ICLR 2025, arXiv 2411.12633; RoboPrompt, ICRA 2025, arXiv 2410.12782; Vid2Robot; the LIBERO-evaluated ICL-with-visual-reasoning paper, arXiv 2603.07530). None runs a sequential protocol or reports forgetting, because the prompt is erased each episode. The binding limit is context: ICRT holds about five trajectories, roughly one task. The one LIBERO number is 54.05% on unseen LIBERO-90 tasks from prompting alone, well below fine-tuned VLAs. Primitive Subspaces (arXiv 2605.30695) shows π₀.₅ has some few-shot capacity without weight updates, which makes "prompt π₀.₅ with support demos" a legitimate zero-update control if wanted.
- **Retrieval-augmented policies.** ReCAP (ICLR 2026, arXiv 2606.15631) is the paper a reviewer will name: frozen policy, no task ID, add a task by adding demos to a pool, already compared against π₀.₅. It runs nested pool-progression studies but never re-measures earlier tasks, requires a shared end-effector action space, and its store grows without bound. RT-Cache (arXiv 2505.09040) quantifies the cost of an append-only store: about 100 GB over Open X-Embodiment and 300 seconds per query for exhaustive search. STRAP (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2412.15182) is on LIBERO-10 but retrieves training data for a fresh per-task policy. Retrieval-VLA (CVPR 2026) **[verify: server refused the PDF]**.
- **Skill libraries.** LOTUS (replay, growth, task-conditioned), Voyager-style code libraries (LLM planner is the task oracle, forgetting never measured), ASPIRE (arXiv 2607.00272, LIBERO-Pro, no forgetting measured). Cite in one paragraph.
- **Hypernetworks.** WIZARD (arXiv 2606.07217) generates LoRA weights for a frozen π₀.₅ from an instruction plus a demo video and reaches 0.09 on held-out LIBERO-10 versus 0.89 for per-task experts; HyperVLA (arXiv 2510.04898) shows instruction conditioning alone suffices for routing on LIBERO and WidowX-SIMPLER. Neither accumulates or measures retention. Together they establish that "no task ID" is not by itself a contribution. Continual hypernetworks (von Oswald et al., ICLR 2020) drop from 99.8% to 69.5% on SplitMNIST when the task oracle is removed.
- **Non-parametric class-incremental** (FeCAM, RanDumb, VINN). RanDumb (arXiv 2402.08823) shows random features plus nearest-class-mean cover 70–100% of the gap to joint training. This is the paper behind "have you tried plain nearest-neighbour over frozen features?". A frozen-π₀.₅-features-plus-kNN control over support demos would answer it.

## 6. Parameter-efficient, prompt, mask and merging families: what transfers to a VLA

**LoRA-subspace methods.** O-LoRA (EMNLP Findings 2023, arXiv 2310.14152) is the canonical task-ID-free, replay-free LoRA baseline: each task's LoRA column space is pushed orthogonal to earlier ones, then merged, so inference cost is constant. OrthoSkillVLA already ran it on a VLA and it reached 30.5% final success (sequential LoRA 32.4, EWC 25.2, KeepLoRA 56.6, their method 83.5). InfLoRA (CVPR 2024) and SD-LoRA (ICLR 2025 oral) are the vision successors; SD-LoRA's "no component selection at inference" property is one we share and CLARE, DMPEL and CORAL do not. "C-LoRA" names two different papers; disambiguate. "CoLoR" and "LoRAContinual" do not appear to exist.

**Prompt-pool methods** (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, S-Prompts, HiDe-Prompt) appear in zero of thirteen continual-robot and continual-VLA papers as baselines. The only policy port is L2M (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2306.14884), an L2P-style modulation pool on a frozen decision transformer; DMPEL ran it on LIBERO and found weak forward transfer "due to its frozen action head", a warning worth engaging with. CODA-Prompt (CVPR 2023) is the direct competitor to the routing claim: differentiable soft attention over a fixed component set, no growth, no task ID. The answers to "isn't your memory a prompt pool with more slots?" are that CODA-Prompt needs the task count known in advance and freezes components per task, whereas our write mask is content-addressed per example, and that HiDe-Prompt stores samples and is not rehearsal-free.

**Mask methods** (PackNet, Piggyback, HAT, SupSup, WSN). All need task ID at inference except SupSup, whose task inference minimises output entropy of a classifier; a flow-matching action head has no output entropy, so SupSup does not transfer to continuous control at all. PackNet has two contradictory published results: it wins Continual World (NeurIPS 2021, twenty Meta-World tasks, performance 0.80 with zero forgetting, beating the multitask oracle at 0.51) when given the task label and trained from scratch, and collapses to 4.8 AUC on LIBERO-Long in CLARE when pruning a frozen pretrained policy. That reconciliation is worth a sentence. WSN has no arXiv preprint **[verify]**.

**Merging.** RETAIN (Levine's group) is sequential weight interpolation on π₀ with our exact pretraining and adaptation splits, with modality-specific mixing weights, and is listed as a baseline in the Simple Recipe paper. MagMax (ECCV 2024), Task Arithmetic, TIES, Localize-and-Stitch (the merging analogue of a sparse write mask) and Model Tailor (whose sensitivity criterion is the closest analogue of our protection term) are the general-purpose citations.

**Mixture-of-experts routing.** Beyond CLARE, DMPEL and Stellar: Emergent Compositional Skills in MoE VLAs (Princeton, arXiv 2607.20771) puts routed LoRA deltas on the action expert's FFN sublayers of π₀ and SmolVLA and evaluates on LIBERO-10. It is multi-task rather than continual, but it is the best published justification for placing sparse modules exactly where our memory layers sit.

## 7. Baselines: what we have, what to add, what to cite instead of running

Across thirteen continual-robot and continual-VLA papers, the baseline frequency is: sequential fine-tuning 13/13, experience replay 13/13, multitask reference 9/13, EWC 7/13, LOTUS 6/13, TAIL with oracle task ID 5/13, PackNet 4/13, sequential LoRA 4/13, prompt-pool methods 0/13.

**Already in the table.** Naive sequential LoRA (r256 at five tasks, r512 at ten, r1216 parameter-matched landing about 4 September), multitask LoRA (r512 at ten tasks; the five-task row is the under-provisioned r32 probe and needs a footnote or a rerun), per-task specialists at r32/r64/r128/r512 (this is TAIL; label it "(oracle task ID)" as DMPEL does), full fine-tuning from raw π₀.₅ and from the libero_90 substrate, zero-shot stage-1 substrate on LIBERO-10 (10.6%, Entry 34; put it in the table).

## 8. Naming the setting

- Under van de Ven and Tolias (arXiv 1904.07734; Nature Machine Intelligence 2022) a language-conditioned policy that always receives the instruction is **task-incremental**, because the instruction supplies task identity. Do not claim domain- or class-incremental; a continual-learning reviewer will catch it. LIBERO itself reports no significant difference between language embeddings and a plain task-ID embedding, which a hostile reviewer can use to say "no task ID" is a distinction without a difference.
- The robotics wording for our constraint is **"continual learning without oracle task identifiers"** (CLARE) and **"exemplar-free"** or **"rehearsal-free"** for the no-replay half. "Task-agnostic" (RWLA, the continual-RL survey) implies unobserved task boundaries during training, which is not our setting. "Task-free" (Aljundi et al., CVPR 2019) means no boundaries at all. Avoid both.
- A usable composite: **exemplar-free continual imitation learning for VLAs without oracle task identifiers, at fixed parameter count.** Every component is attested. Invent nothing.
- The precise distinction to write: the claim is not that the instruction is withheld, it is that **no task-conditional parameter selection** occurs; a single shared parameter set with no per-task heads, no oracle index, no router that grows.
- One finding from our own log to handle carefully: Entry 28 showed the expert router's routing is scene-driven, with the language pathway near-inert at inference (though load-bearing at warm-up, Entry 54). LIBERO-Plus (arXiv 2510.13626) reports VLAs are "largely insensitive to language variations". Frame routing-by-observation as a strength (the policy does not need the instruction to find the right slots), and consider an instruction-perturbation control on one task.

## 9. Metrics and protocol

- **Headline forgetting must be success-rate based.** LIBERO's Appendix E.2 plots loss matrices and concludes that behaviour-cloning loss "can be a misleading indicator of task success rate". No paper reports an MSE forgetting matrix as a headline. We already have four-seed rollout retention triangles (Entry 64); make those the headline, keep the paired-noise MSE matrix as a validated cheap proxy in the supplement, and cite LIBERO E.2 ourselves. The Liu et al. paper explicitly leaves "a more systematic study of forgetting metrics to future work", which is an invitation.
- **Use LIBERO's definitions** (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, arXiv 2306.03310): NBT_k = 1/(K−k) Σ_{τ>k} (c_{k,k} − c_{τ,k}), averaged over k; AUC per their Eq. 3; FWT is their mean over the learning curve and is not comparable if the epoch schedule differs (DMPEL flags this). Final average success as AVG = (1/T) Σ_j S_{T,j} (origin: ACC in Lopez-Paz and Ranzato, GEM, NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1706.08840). Note the sign convention: Zhu et al. use BWT with the opposite sign.
- **Also report** normalised NBT (Liu et al., App. A.2, each term scaled by c_{k,k}, because absolute NBT flatters weak policies), and a zero-shot held-out-task column (ZS in the Simple Recipe paper, App. B), which a frozen-backbone method should win.
- **Protocol pitfalls.** Task ordering: LIBERO found significant variation across orderings; either run a second order or state and justify the fixed one (Liu et al. use the same order throughout). Stability gap (De Lange, van de Ven, Tuytelaars, ICLR 2023, arXiv 2205.13452): transient drops on old tasks mid-block are invisible to end-of-block evaluation; one intra-block evaluation curve on one task closes it. Hyperparameter budget (Cha and Cho, TMLR, arXiv 2403.09066): state how baseline hyperparameters were tuned and use the same budget; the Simple Recipe paper's "local sweep within one order of magnitude" is the sentence to copy. Label every oracle row "(oracle task ID)".
- **Real robot.** The five-task sequential real-robot shape is well precedented (CLARE 5 on Franka FR3, LifelongVLA 5 on xArm, PPL 5 on Panda, Zhu et al. 10 on Piper). No continual work exists on WidowX. Every real-world number we have is function-space; the papers above all report rollouts, and Zhu et al. use a stepwise 0–100 rubric rather than binary success, which is worth adopting. Drift-to-success conversion without rollouts is the gap a reviewer will name.
- **LIBERO-40** (CLARE's concatenation of all four suites, forty tasks) is becoming the scalability stress test for methods claiming constant parameter cost. Not for this deadline, but note it.
